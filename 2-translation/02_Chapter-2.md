>[!summary] English Text
>Scientific American published Popper's confessions and those of other researchers under an article
>titled: "Darwin Under Doubt: Why a Group of Non-Religious Scientists Doubt
>the Theory of Evolution?"
>
>Popper confirmed his position on the theory and on natural selection in detail in his last book,
>written one year before his death: *Evolutionary Epistemology*. In this book, Popper
>cited confessions from leading evolutionary scientists throughout its history (56).
>
>He quoted one of the giants of Darwinism—on his own description—
>C. H. Waddington:
>who stated that "natural selection is nothing more than a tautology."
>
>A tautology means: a repetition; mere verbiage. So natural selection, which is the backbone
>of evolutionary theory, is not constrained by evidence, and is not a scientific proposition,
>but merely a repetition of words—like pouring water over water after the effort!

>[!summary] English Text>
>But why is natural selection a verbal repetition?
>Because any living organism you assume was "naturally selected" merely because it survived.
>• Mere verbiage.
>So what does it mean to say: "What occurred was selection"?
>Why couldn't something else have preserved this organism?
>• Whatever organism survives, we would say: it was selected!
>• These are speculative assumptions, not scientific observations!
>
>Popper continues: The same view as Waddington is held by
>Ronald Fisher, founder of modern Darwinism, where Fisher also declares that natural selection is a tautology.
>
>(56) *Evolutionary Epistemology, Rationality, and the Sociology of Knowledge*, with Contributions by Sir Karl Popper.
